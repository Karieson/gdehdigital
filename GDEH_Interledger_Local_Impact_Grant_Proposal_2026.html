1. Executive Summary

Garissa Digital Empowerment Hub (GDEH) proposes a six-month community-led initiative to improve understanding, practical use and local experimentation around open payments and inclusive digital financial infrastructure in Garissa County, Kenya.

Many young people and small community businesses increasingly depend on mobile and digital platforms but have limited opportunities to understand how modern payment systems, digital financial services, interoperability, open standards and related digital infrastructure can expand access to economic opportunities. GDEH will create a practical community learning environment where participants can learn, discuss, demonstrate and experiment with open and inclusive digital payment concepts.

The project will combine community conversations, practical workshops, demonstrations, research and storytelling, a youth innovation sprint, and development of accessible learning materials. The initiative will particularly encourage young people and community-based entrepreneurs to explore how open payment ecosystems can support small businesses, digital livelihoods and broader financial inclusion.

Core proposition: GDEH will act as a local community bridge between underserved digital users and the emerging ecosystem of open, inclusive and interoperable digital financial infrastructure.

3. Community Problem & Opportunity

Garissa County has a large youth population and a growing dependence on mobile technology, online services and digital commerce. GDEH's existing youth empowerment work has identified practical barriers including limited access to computers and reliable internet, inadequate practical digital skills, limited exposure to technology innovation and limited access to professional networks and digital economic opportunities.

The broader challenge is not only access to devices. Community members also need the knowledge and confidence to understand the systems behind digital transactions, the opportunities created by more open and interoperable payment infrastructure, and the responsible use of digital financial services.

GDEH therefore proposes a focused community learning and experimentation programme that translates complex concepts around open payments and digital financial infrastructure into practical local learning, demonstrations and community-led innovation.

4. Overall Goal

To strengthen community knowledge, participation and experimentation around open payments and inclusive digital financial infrastructure, enabling underserved young people and community businesses in Garissa to better understand and use emerging digital financial opportunities.

5. Specific Objectives

  1. Provide practical learning sessions on open payments, digital financial infrastructure and inclusive digital finance.
  2. Create a community space for conversations and demonstrations involving youth, entrepreneurs, technology practitioners and other local stakeholders.
  3. Support youth participants to identify local financial-access problems and develop early-stage technology or community solutions through an innovation sprint.
  4. Produce simple, accessible learning and awareness materials that explain open payments and digital financial infrastructure in locally relevant terms.
  5. Document community experiences, opportunities and barriers and share learning with the wider ecosystem.

6. Target Community & Inclusion

Primary participants will include:

  • Young people interested in technology, entrepreneurship and innovation.
  • Emerging digital entrepreneurs and small business operators.
  • Community members with limited exposure to digital financial systems.
  • Young women and participants from underserved communities.
  • Technology learners, innovators and community digital champions.

The project will use accessible community sessions and practical demonstrations so that participation does not depend on advanced technical knowledge.

7. Key Project Activities

A. Community Conversations on Open Payments

Facilitated conversations will introduce participants to open payments, digital financial infrastructure, interoperability, open standards and the relevance of these concepts to local businesses and digital livelihoods.

B. Practical Workshops & Demonstrations

GDEH will conduct practical learning sessions using examples, demonstrations and guided exercises. Topics will include digital payment ecosystems, financial access, responsible digital transactions, open payment concepts and opportunities for community innovation.

C. Youth Digital Finance Innovation Sprint

A participatory innovation sprint will bring together youth to identify local financial-access challenges and develop early-stage concepts, prototypes, content or community solutions. Selected ideas will receive peer feedback and mentorship.

D. Community Research & Storytelling

GDEH will document community experiences and identify barriers, opportunities and practical lessons relating to digital payments and financial access. Findings will be converted into a concise community learning report and stories.

E. Open Learning Materials & Awareness Campaign

The project will produce accessible digital and printable materials for community use, including explainers, workshop resources, practical guides and awareness content focused on open payments and digital financial infrastructure.

F. Local Ecosystem Gathering

A closing community gathering will bring participants and relevant local technology, business and community stakeholders together to share lessons, demonstrate outputs and identify pathways for continued collaboration.

15. Sustainability & Continuity

The project is designed as a foundation for continued community learning rather than a one-time event. GDEH will retain and reuse the learning materials, community research and workshop methodology developed during the six-month period.

Following the grant, GDEH will continue community digital learning through affordable digital services, youth membership activities, partnerships, volunteer mentors, technology-sector relationships and revenue-generating digital services. The existing GDEH model also emphasizes access to computers, internet, mentorship and practical digital opportunities for underserved youth.

The project will encourage participants who develop promising concepts to continue working with GDEH and relevant ecosystem partners beyond the grant period.

17. Conclusion

Garissa Digital Empowerment Hub seeks support to establish a practical community learning and innovation programme that connects underserved young people and community businesses with the opportunities emerging from open payments and inclusive digital financial infrastructure.

With a focused six-month programme, a modest USD 3,000 investment can support community conversations, practical workshops, an innovation sprint, local research, open learning materials and an ecosystem gathering. The project will generate reusable knowledge and locally grounded learning that can continue through GDEH after the grant period.

GDEH is committed to practical learning, inclusion, youth ownership, responsible technology use, community participation and measurable outcomes.
