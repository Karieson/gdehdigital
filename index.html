About GDEH

Garissa Digital Empowerment Hub (GDEH) is a community-based organization committed to bridging the digital divide in Garissa County. The organization focuses on equipping youth, women, and the wider community with practical digital and entrepreneurial skills that improve livelihoods and promote self-reliance.

Formation of Garissa Digital Empowerment Hub (GDEH) CBO

The Garissa Digital Empowerment Hub (GDEH) Community-Based Organization (CBO) was formally conceptualized and formed in early 2026 in Garissa Township, Garissa County, Kenya. It was established as a response to the growing digital inequality affecting youth, women, and persons with disabilities (PWDs) across Garissa and the wider Northern Kenya region.

Reason for Formation

The CBO was created after recognizing several critical challenges in the region:

a) Digital Divide
Many young people in Garissa County lacked:
Basic computer literacy
Access to digital learning opportunities
Exposure to modern ICT tools and platforms
This created a widening gap between urban digital development and rural communities.

b) Economic Challenges
High youth unemployment
Limited access to online job markets and digital entrepreneurship opportunities
Dependence on informal and low-income economic activities

c) Low Technological Awareness
Limited awareness of ICT as a career path
Lack of structured training centers for digital skills
Minimal integration of technology in daily community activities

d) Social Inclusion Gaps
Persons with disabilities had even fewer opportunities to access ICT training
Women and marginalized youth were underrepresented in digital programs

Vision of GDEH CBO

The organization was established with a long-term vision to:
Bridge the digital divide in Garissa County and Northern Kenya
Equip youth with practical ICT and entrepreneurial skills
Promote digital literacy and innovation
Enhance socio-economic development through technology
Foster inclusive growth for all community groups

Conclusion

The Garissa Digital Empowerment Hub (GDEH) CBO was born out of a real and urgent need to address digital inequality, unemployment, and lack of ICT awareness in Garissa County. Driven by a group of passionate young leaders led by Abdullahi Sheikh Aden Karie and Samira Abdi Maalim, the organization stands as a community-based solution aimed at transforming lives through digital skills, empowerment, and inclusive development across Northern Kenya.

Founding Members and Leadership

The initiative was founded by a group of 10 committed members, led by:

Abdullahi Sheikh Aden Karie
IT Diploma Graduate
Undergraduate student at Mount Kenya University
IT Support Technician with diverse ICT experience
Trained in leadership through the YALI Network
Completed Humanitarian Aid training via Coursera
He was driven by firsthand experience of the digital gap in Garissa County, where he observed:
Lack of access to ICT infrastructure
Low digital literacy among youth
Slow economic growth due to limited technology adoption
His vision was to create a community-driven platform that empowers youth through practical digital skills and entrepreneurship training.

Samira Abdi Maalim
Young female leader passionate about socio-economic development
Holder of a Certificate in Accounting and Accountability short course training
Advocate for youth and women empowerment
Strong supporter of inclusive education and community growth
She played a key role in promoting gender inclusion, accountability, and community engagement within the CBO.

Other Founding Members
The organization was also supported by 8 additional co-founders, each contributing skills in:
Community mobilization
ICT awareness
Youth mentorship
Administration and coordination
Local development initiatives

Key Factors Influencing Formation

Several factors influenced the establishment of GDEH CBO:

a) Regional Digital Inequality
Northern Kenya, particularly Garissa Township, had limited ICT infrastructure compared to other parts of the country.

b) Youth Unemployment Crisis
A large number of educated and semi-educated youth lacked employable digital skills.

c) Desire for Community Transformation
The founders aimed to shift the community from dependency to self-reliance through technology and innovation.

d) Leadership and Exposure
The founding members had exposure to:
ICT training and certifications
Leadership programs (such as YALI)
Online learning platforms (Coursera and others)
This exposure motivated them to replicate similar opportunities locally.

e) Inclusion of Marginalized Groups
A strong motivation was to ensure:
Youth inclusion
Women empowerment
Accessibility for Persons with Disabilities (PWDs)

Internet browsing and safe online practices
Email creation and communication
Using digital platforms (Google tools, social media, online forms)

Opens doors to online learning and jobs

Online communication skills

Business planning and entrepreneurship skills
Agricultural management skills
Financial and record-keeping skills
