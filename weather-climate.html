Understanding Weather

Important weather information for farmers

These weather elements can influence farm activities, crop development and agricultural productivity.

01 — RAINFALL

Rainfall Patterns

Rainfall amount, timing and distribution influence soil moisture, planting decisions, water availability and crop development. A short period of rain does not necessarily mean that enough water is available for the entire growing season.

02 — TEMPERATURE

Temperature Changes

High temperatures can increase water loss from soil and plants. Temperature conditions also influence germination, flowering and crop development.

03 — WIND

Wind Conditions

Strong winds can damage crops, increase evaporation and affect irrigation. Wind conditions should also be considered before any pesticide application.

04 — HUMIDITY

Air Humidity

Humidity influences moisture conditions around plants and can affect the development of certain crop diseases.

05 — DROUGHT

Drought Conditions

Extended periods of insufficient rainfall can reduce soil moisture, affect crop growth and place pressure on agricultural water supplies.

06 — EXTREME EVENTS

Heavy Rain and Flooding

Intense rainfall can cause waterlogging, soil erosion and damage to farms. Farmers should consider drainage and appropriate flood preparedness.

Agricultural Planning

Plan your farm around seasonal conditions

Agricultural decisions should consider reliable seasonal forecasts, local rainfall observations, available water and advice from agricultural extension officers.

Before the Planting Season

Prepare your farm and review available weather information before committing resources to planting.

  • Review official seasonal rainfall advisories.
  • Prepare land according to soil and crop requirements.
  • Check seed quality and availability.
  • Assess water availability and irrigation options.
  • Develop a realistic farm production plan.

During the Growing Season

Monitor crop development and adjust farm activities according to changing weather and soil conditions.

  • Monitor rainfall and soil moisture.
  • Manage irrigation according to crop needs.
  • Watch for signs of drought or waterlogging.
  • Inspect crops for pests and diseases.
  • Record farm observations and activities.

During Dry Conditions

Plan carefully when rainfall is limited and water resources become more difficult to access.

  • Prioritise efficient water use.
  • Use mulching where suitable.
  • Maintain water harvesting structures.
  • Consider suitable drought-tolerant crops.
  • Seek advice before changing planting plans.

Before and During Heavy Rainfall

Prepare for possible runoff, flooding and excessive water accumulation around crops.

  • Inspect drainage channels.
  • Protect exposed soil against erosion.
  • Secure farm equipment and supplies.
  • Monitor waterlogged areas.
  • Follow official flood and safety advisories.
Climate Risk Awareness

Climate-related challenges in farming

Farmers in arid and semi-arid environments may face several interconnected climate challenges. Understanding these risks helps communities prepare and adapt.

Prolonged Dry Periods

Extended periods without sufficient rainfall can reduce crop productivity and limit water availability.

Possible response

Improve water conservation, consider suitable crop choices and follow seasonal agricultural advice.

Unpredictable Rainfall

Changes in rainfall timing and distribution can make it difficult to plan planting and other farm activities.

Possible response

Follow updated official forecasts and avoid relying on assumptions about rainfall dates.

High Temperatures

High temperatures may increase evaporation and create heat stress for crops and livestock.

Possible response

Improve soil moisture conservation and use appropriate shade and water management practices.

Soil Erosion

Heavy rainfall and surface runoff can remove fertile topsoil and damage agricultural land.

Possible response

Maintain suitable ground cover, manage runoff and use appropriate soil conservation practices.

Water Shortages

Limited access to water can affect irrigation, household needs and livestock production.

Possible response

Explore rainwater harvesting, efficient irrigation and responsible water storage.

Crop and Livestock Stress

Changing climate conditions may increase pressure on crops, pasture and livestock resources.

Possible response

Strengthen farm planning and seek local agricultural and livestock extension support.

Building Agricultural Resilience

Climate-smart agricultural practices

Climate-smart agriculture promotes practices that improve agricultural productivity, strengthen resilience and support responsible management of natural resources.

Soil Moisture Conservation

Use appropriate mulching, ground cover and soil management techniques to reduce unnecessary moisture loss.

Efficient Irrigation

Consider suitable irrigation methods that deliver water efficiently and reduce avoidable losses.

Water Harvesting

Explore appropriate rainwater harvesting and storage systems to improve water availability.

Suitable Crop Selection

Choose crops and varieties that are appropriate for local soil, water availability and seasonal conditions.

Agroforestry and Tree Planting

Where suitable, integrate trees into farming systems to support land protection and environmental resilience.

Farm Diversification

Consider suitable diversification strategies to reduce dependence on a single agricultural activity.
